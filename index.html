What Should an Invoice Include?

A typical invoice identifies the seller and customer, gives a unique invoice number, states the issue date and due date, describes the goods or services, lists quantities and prices, and shows the total due. Applicable tax details and payment instructions should also be included. Exact requirements vary by country, business type and transaction.

How to Set Payment Terms

State the agreed payment deadline clearly. Depending on the agreement, a business may request payment on receipt or within a specified number of days. Make sure the terms match the contract and any applicable law. Keep records of invoices sent and payments received.

How to Save an Invoice as a PDF

Complete the invoice and select “Print / Save as PDF”. In the browser's print window, choose “Save as PDF” if available, check the preview, and save the file to a secure location. Print layouts may differ between devices.

How to Keep Invoice Records

Store copies of issued invoices, supporting documents and payment records securely. Browser storage is useful for a local draft, but it is not a cloud backup. Keep separate records and follow the retention requirements that apply to your business.

Worked Invoice Calculation

This fictional example shows how a discount and illustrative tax rate affect the final amount.

Service A: £100.00

Service B: £50.00

Subtotal: £100 + £50 = £150.00

Discount: 10% of £150 = £15.00

Amount after discount: £150 − £15 = £135.00

Illustrative tax: 20% of £135 = £27.00

Total: £135 + £27 = £162.00

This example is for arithmetic only. The tax rate, whether tax should be charged, and the order of calculations must follow the rules for your location and transaction. SwiftInvoice does not determine your tax obligations or replace professional accounting advice.

Check the Numbers Before Sending

  • Confirm that every quantity and unit price is correct.
  • Check that the discount does not exceed the subtotal.
  • Use the appropriate tax rate and calculation method.
  • Verify the final amount and payment terms.
  • Keep a copy of the invoice for your records.
