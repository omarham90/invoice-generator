Invoice guides for small businesses

Understand the information to include and how to check your totals before sending a document.

What should a professional invoice include?

A professional invoice records what was supplied, who supplied it, who is being billed and how much is due. Clear information helps both parties understand the transaction and makes record keeping easier.

Essential invoice details

  • Business information: your business name and relevant contact details.
  • Client information: the name of the person or business being billed.
  • Invoice number: a unique reference that helps you track the document.
  • Dates: the invoice issue date and payment due date, where applicable.
  • Itemised charges: clear descriptions, quantities, unit prices and line totals.
  • Adjustments: any applicable tax, discount, shipping or other agreed charges.
  • Payment instructions: how and when the client should pay.

Requirements differ by country, business type and transaction. Check the rules that apply to you, especially if you must include a tax registration number or prescribed tax information.

How to calculate an invoice total

Start by multiplying each item's quantity by its unit price. Add the line totals to find the subtotal. Apply the relevant tax rate, add shipping or other agreed charges, and subtract any applicable discount.

Example calculation

  • Three hours of work at $40 per hour: $120.00
  • One service charge: $30.00
  • Subtotal: $150.00
  • Tax at 10%, assuming it applies to the full subtotal: $15.00
  • Discount: $5.00
  • Amount due: $160.00

Tax rules can vary, including whether a discount is deducted before or after tax. This generator uses a simple calculation and does not determine your legal tax obligations. Confirm the correct treatment for your jurisdiction.

Invoice, receipt or estimate: what is the difference?

Invoice

An invoice requests payment for products or services supplied, according to the agreed terms. It normally identifies the charges and the amount due.

Receipt

A receipt generally confirms that a payment has been received. Update the document's wording and payment status to reflect the actual transaction before issuing it.

Estimate or quote

An estimate or quotation sets out proposed charges before work begins or a purchase is completed. Make the validity period, scope and conditions clear where appropriate.
