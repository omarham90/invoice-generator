How to Create a Professional Invoice

An invoice records the products or services you have provided and tells your customer how much they need to pay. A clear, accurate invoice helps you keep organised records and makes payment expectations easier to understand.

What should an invoice include?

A professional invoice will usually include the following information:

  • Your business name and contact details.
  • Your customer's name or business details.
  • A unique invoice number.
  • The invoice date and payment due date.
  • A clear description of each product or service.
  • Quantity, unit price and line totals.
  • Applicable taxes, discounts and the final amount due.
  • Payment instructions and any relevant payment terms.

Requirements differ by country, business type and transaction. Check the invoicing and tax rules that apply to your situation.

How to create an invoice in six steps

1. Add your business details

Start with the name and contact information your customer needs to identify your business. Include any additional details required by the rules in your country.

2. Enter your customer's details

Use the correct customer or company name and any required billing information. Check spelling and addresses before sending the invoice.

3. Give the invoice a unique number

Assign each invoice a number that helps you find it later. A consistent numbering system makes record keeping easier. Avoid accidentally reusing an existing invoice number.

4. Describe your products or services

List each item separately and describe the work or product clearly. Add the quantity and price so the customer can understand the charges.

5. Check taxes, discounts and totals

Calculate the subtotal, apply any appropriate discount, and include the correct tax treatment for the transaction. Tax rules vary, so confirm the applicable rate and calculation method before issuing the invoice.

6. Set payment terms and save the invoice

State when payment is due and include suitable payment instructions. Review every figure, then save a copy for your records and provide the invoice to your customer.

Helpful tip: Before sending an invoice, double-check the customer details, invoice number, due date, calculations and payment instructions.

How do you calculate an invoice total?

A basic invoice calculation looks like this:

  • Line total = quantity × unit price.
  • Subtotal = the sum of all line totals.
  • Tax = the taxable amount × the applicable tax rate.
  • Final total = subtotal − discount + applicable tax.

For example, three items costing £20 each give a subtotal of £60. If a 10% tax rate applies to the full £60 and there is no discount, tax is £6 and the total is £66. This is an illustration only; actual tax treatment depends on the transaction and local rules.

What is the difference between an invoice and a receipt?

An invoice requests payment for goods or services. A receipt generally confirms that payment has been made. Keep both when appropriate, as they serve different purposes in your records.

Do I need to charge tax on every invoice?

Not necessarily. Whether you must charge tax depends on your location, registration status, the customer and the transaction. Consult your local tax authority or a qualified adviser if unsure.

When should an invoice be paid?

The due date and payment terms should be stated clearly on the invoice. Agree on suitable terms with your customer and follow any applicable legal requirements.

Should I keep copies of invoices?

Keeping organised copies helps with bookkeeping, payment tracking and tax records. Retention periods and requirements vary by country.
